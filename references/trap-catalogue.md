# Trap Catalogue

Every entry: **Signal** (how it shows up in conversation) → **Why it's a trap** → **Counter-move** → example flag line.

Read only the domain(s) relevant to the moment. Do not dump this list at the user.

---

## A. Focus & time

**A1. Tooling accumulation** *(the most common builder pattern)*
Signal: "let's set up …", new skill suites, new folders, new dashboards, before the current deliverable exists.
Why: setup feels like progress because it produces artifacts. It produces zero results. It is the most comfortable form of procrastination for builders.
Counter: one piece of setup per project, then the project's first shippable artifact. Everything else parked.
Flag: `⚠️ Flag: this is the third setup move this week and nothing is built — cap structure here and ship the smallest artifact — add it or park it?`

**A2. Parallel projects**
Signal: 3+ things marked "in progress"; "we can do both".
Why: attention is not divisible. The number of unfinished projects is a direct proxy for how many will finish.
Counter: name the primary for this month. Everything else is explicitly parked, with a date to revisit.
Flag: `⚠️ Flag: X, Y, Z are all "in progress" — none gets the hours needed — park two, name one — which?`

**A3. The exciting option**
Signal: choosing the most ambitious/novel option when a boring one exists.
Why: excitement scales with scope. Ambition feels like commitment but is usually just novelty-seeking.
Counter: choose the option that can be finished; save ambition for game 2+.
Flag: `⚠️ Flag: picking the exciting option over the finishable one — swap to the small version, bank the ambition — confirm?`

**A4. Reversible-looking commitments**
Signal: "just one quick call", "a small favour", recurring meetings, free "just this once" work.
Why: they consume the same hours as paid work and are socially expensive to unwind.
Counter: decide in advance what recurring slots exist this month; anything new trades against them.

**A5. Planning as a substitute for thinking**
Signal: long documents written before any evidence is collected.
Why: docs encode assumptions and make them feel settled.
Counter: one page max before data; expand docs only after evidence.

## B. Scope & building

**B1. MVP inflation**
Signal: "and it should also have…" during build; features added before first launch.
Why: every feature adds risk to the parts that already work. Launch is the only teacher.
Counter: freeze REQUIREMENTS; new ideas go to a Later list.
Flag: `⚠️ Flag: scope added before the first publish — MVP is frozen at [X], this goes to Later — park it?`

**B2. Building frameworks instead of the thing**
Signal: generic systems, abstraction layers, "so we can reuse it later".
Why: reusable infrastructure pays off on the 3rd use; you have 0 shipped.
Counter: hardcode the first version. Extract later, if it still matters.

**B3. Premature polish**
Signal: art, animation, sound design, lore writing before retention is measured.
Why: polish is expensive and unvalidated. It cannot fix a boring loop.
Counter: grey boxes until D1 clears benchmark; then polish the parts the data says matter.

**B4. The unkillable project**
Signal: a project past its kill date with no new external evidence; "it just needs one more update".
Why: sunk cost wearing a productive mask.
Counter: honor the written kill condition, or write a new one with a hard date — but write it now.
Flag: `⚠️ Flag: past kill date with no new evidence — either kill it or write a new condition with a date — pick one today?`

**B5. Tech novelty**
Signal: choosing a new engine/stack/dependency because it's interesting.
Why: novelty burns the hours meant for the product and creates migration debt.
Counter: boring stack until a specific limitation forces a change.

## C. Money & business

**C1. Spending before validation**
Signal: ads, commissioned art, domains, subscriptions, upload fees before any retention data.
Why: money buys attention, not product-market fit. It amplifies whatever is already there (usually nothing).
Counter: gate spending behind a measurable result (retention, conversion, first revenue).
Flag: `⚠️ Flag: spend before evidence — gate it behind [metric] — spend now or after 2 weeks of data?`

**C2. Hoping the platform pays**
Signal: revenue maths built on platform payouts (ads RPM, DevEx rates, affiliate) with no traffic yet.
Why: payout rates are per-mille of an audience that does not exist yet; rates also change unilaterally.
Counter: model the required audience first ("X clicks/day at Y RPM = Z"), then judge whether that's reachable.

**C3. Free work with a revenue promise**
Signal: "exposure", "we'll make it up later", unpaid builds.
Why: exposure is not a customer; promises are not contracts.
Counter: price it or decline; at minimum cap it in hours with written scope.

**C4. Buying the "done" feeling**
Signal: new hardware, course, subscription, theme purchase timed with low motivation.
Why: shopping is motivation's cheap substitute.
Counter: one 48-hour rule on any non-essential purchase; ship something first.

## D. Data & accounts

**D1. No backup of irreplaceable work** *(see Roblox pack R4)*
Signal: only one copy of something that took weeks.
Why: storage failure and accidental overwrite are the only causes that matter, and they're binary.
Counter: export/copy at milestones; test that a restore actually opens.

**D2. ToS-risky automation** *(accounts that hold money)*
Signal: automating a platform that punishes automation, on the account that holds money.
Why: one flag can cost the account permanently; some accounts are irreplaceable (one per lifetime).
Counter: separate, disposable identity for automation; never the money account.

**D3. Credential exposure**
Signal: keys in chat/logs/screenshots/repos; sharing tokens to "save time".
Why: exposure is permanent; rotation is the only fix and it's expensive.
Counter: store in files with 600 perms, never inline; rotate anything that ever appeared in a log or screenshot.

**D4. Irreversible deletions on incomplete information**
Signal: "let's clean everything", mass deletes, wiping caches/configs mid-project.
Why: disk space is cheap; lost context is not.
Counter: move to a `_to_delete/` folder, delete after 7 days.

## E. Content & audience

**E1. Broadcasting before understanding**
Signal: posting/launching at volume without checking what the audience responds to.
Why: volume without feedback is just noise accumulation; it also trains the algorithm on the wrong signal.
Counter: publish small, read metrics, then scale what works.

**E2. Trend chasing late** *(see Roblox pack R2)*
Signal: "this is hot right now" with no check of when it became hot.
Why: by the time a trend is visible to everyone, the derivative wave is already peaking.
Counter: check the chart's *change* column, not its position; only enter rising curves.

**E3. Fighting for the same audience as an established giant**
Signal: "like X but better" aimed at a category leader.
Why: incumbents win on accumulated content, not quality. Head-on competition is a gift to them.
Counter: find the underserved sub-niche or adjacent mechanic.

## F. Learning & skill

**F1. Tutorial loops**
Signal: consuming course after course without building.
Why: recognition is not skill; only recall under constraints is.
Counter: one tutorial, then immediately build the same thing from memory.

**F2. Learning order inversion**
Signal: studying advanced topics before the basics are used in anger.
Why: unanchored knowledge evaporates; advanced topics only stick after the basic version hurts.
Counter: learn the next thing the current project needs. That's the whole curriculum.

**F3. Coping instead of demanding**
Signal: saying "yes" to everything to avoid conflict; avoiding clients/feedback that might sting.
Why: unclear boundaries and unasked questions both cost more later than now.
Counter: script one honest sentence for the next uncomfortable moment.

## R. Roblox / game-dev pack

**R1. Big first game**
Signal: first project is an RPG/open world/multiplayer fighter.
Why: those are 6-24 month projects needing skills the team doesn't have yet; the first game's job is to teach the pipeline.
Counter: first game = smallest loop that can publish in ≤2 weeks.

**R2. Entering a trope after peak**
Signal: building a "steal X" clone or similar because it's on the front page.
Why: derivative games win by arriving early in a wave; late arrivals inherit a dead trend.
Counter: check the biggest-gainers list and 7-day change before committing; if the originator is declining, skip.

**R3. Pure clone**
Signal: same loop, no mechanical or thematic difference.
Why: players choose the original; the algorithm punishes low differentiation by starving impressions.
Counter: one clear sentence: "same loop, but ___". If missing, don't build.

**R4. Only-one-copy place files**
Signal: weeks of work living only in Studio's cloud place.
Why: accepted/exposed places can be taken down; accounts can be lost; local edits can overwrite.
Counter: `.rbxl` export to `Places/` at every session end and every milestone.

**R5. Publishing without analytics**
Signal: "let's launch and see".
Why: "seeing" requires instrumentation; without metrics a launch teaches nothing and the 14-day gate cannot be judged.
Counter: analytics + funnel events live before publish; check 24h/72h/day-7.

**R6. Client-trusting economy**
Signal: Robux/items/currency granted by client-side code or unvalidated remotes.
Why: exploiters find it within hours; the game's economy and reputation die first.
Counter: every currency/inventory change validated server-side from the first commit.

**R7. Content volume as a moat (RP/social, giant maps)**
Signal: choosing a genre whose winner has thousands of hours of accumulated content.
Why: you can't out-content an incumbent studio in 2 weeks.
Counter: pick a genre where polish and a twist beat volume.
